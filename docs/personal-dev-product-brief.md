# Personal Development Product — Working Brief

Status: **brainstorm**. Nothing built. Captured so the thinking survives the session.

## Origin

Take Nightmagic's mechanism — narrated recording in, structured artifact out — and
point it at a B2C personal development audience instead of a B2B operations buyer.

## The reframe

B2B buyers want the work to go away: labor replaced, cost removed. A personal
development audience wants the opposite — their whole identity is discipline and
effort. Selling them automation sells them laziness.

The B2C promise is **capture, not automation**: everything you know is trapped in
your head, and recording is how it gets out.

## The audience (this is the real asset)

Mailing to two lists at once, ~500K total reach:

- **Brian Tracy (~300K)** — 45–70, sales pros, managers, consultants, small business
  owners. Direct-response conditioned. Bought *Eat That Frog*, *Goals!*,
  *The Psychology of Selling*. Want their expertise **systematized**.
- **Jack Canfield (~200K)** — skews more coach, speaker, healer; more female.
  *Chicken Soup for the Soul* (500M copies), *The Success Principles*. Canfield has
  spent thirty years teaching people to become authors. Want their message **shared**.

The intersection of those two audiences is one person: someone with something
valuable inside them that has never made it out of their head. Neither list is
technical, which makes the one-question-at-a-time voice interview essential rather
than a nice-to-have.

## The offer: "Your Life's Work"

You talk. An AI ghostwriter interviews you one question at a time. You walk out with
a named signature methodology, a book manuscript, a course outline, and lead magnets —
in your own voice.

Positioning line: *You don't write it. You don't type it. You talk, and an AI
ghostwriter interviews you like a real one would.* Both audiences already understand
what a ghostwriter is and already value one in the thousands.

### Directions considered and set aside

| Direction | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Personal operating system (routines + accountability) | Lands in the productivity-app graveyard; value hard to prove |
| Delegation machine (SOPs for a VA) | B2B-lite, emotionally flat, hard to run a big promo on |
| Legacy / memoir capture | Real market, wrong energy — bought for your father, not yourself |

## Pricing and structure

**Recommendation: $997 front end, sold from a webinar**, with a $3–5K done-with-you
ascension.

Rough brackets at ~500K reach with two heavyweight endorsements:

| Price | Est. sales | Est. gross |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $97–197 impulse | 1,000–2,500 | $150K–370K |
| $497–997 webinar | 500–1,500 | $350K–$1M |
| $2K+ application funnel | 250–500 | $500K–$1M (needs a sales team) |

Ranges, not promises — conversion lives or dies on the funnel.

Why $997 and not low-ticket: both audiences buy at exactly that price from exactly
that format; it supports the ascension where promos like this actually get rich; and
unlike a normal info product this one carries **real marginal cost** (transcription +
LLM inference per buyer). Assuming a 50% JV split, a $97 sale leaves ~$48 against
maybe $10 of compute. At $997 you keep ~$500 a head.

## Sell an event, not software access

Wrap it as a **live 3-day intensive** where the software is the vehicle.

- Matches how this audience buys
- Deadline drives completion, and completion generates the testimonials that fund
  the next launch
- **Controls concurrency.** 1,200 buyers hitting an unproven AI pipeline on the same
  Tuesday is the single most likely way this fails in public, in front of Tracy and
  Canfield. A scheduled cohort lets humans backstop the gaps and ships a 70%-built
  product that looks finished.

## One product, two emails

- **Tracy angle:** thirty years of hard-won expertise, finally turned into a signature
  methodology you can teach, license, and sell.
- **Canfield angle:** the book you've been meaning to write for twenty years — talked
  out in a single weekend.

## Known risk

"Become an author/expert" is crowded and slightly disreputable — self-publishing gurus
have burned this audience before. Everyone else sells *teaching* you to write the book.
The entire differentiation is that this teaches nothing: you talk, and the thing gets
built from your own words. Finish the weekend with a real manuscript and you're a
category of one. Finish with a template and homework and you're the fourteenth guy.

## Open questions
